A little about me

Following the light at the edge of galaxies.

Hi! I am Giulia, originally from Belluno in the Dolomites of northern Italy. After growing up in Verona and studying in Bologna and Padua, I completed my PhD in Tenerife before moving to Sydney, Australia.

I am a Research Associate at the University of New South Wales, working with Professor Sarah Brough. My work combines deep imaging, astronomical surveys, advanced data reduction, and quantitative analysis to bring extremely faint structures into view.

Research

Reading the Universe in its faintest details.

Diffuse structures can be thousands of times fainter than the night sky. Preserving them requires careful observations and specialised analysis.

01

Galaxy edges

Developing robust methods to identify and characterise the gradual transition between galaxies and their surrounding space.

02

Dwarf galaxies

Using faint systems and almost-dark galaxies as laboratories for galaxy formation and dark matter.

03

Stellar halos & streams

Tracing the remnants of mergers, accretion, and satellite disruption in the outskirts of galaxies.

04

Intracluster light

Studying stars that are no longer bound to individual galaxies and what they reveal about cluster assembly.

Outreach and public engagement

Outreach & service

Making astronomy part of the conversation.

I believe astronomy should be accessible, engaging, and connected to the wider community. I have contributed to planetarium tours, stargazing events, public talks, school activities, online events, and science communication publications in Italy, Spain, and Australia.

I am also a member of the ASA Early Career Researcher Chapter steering committee and run the ASA ECR Histories and Mysteries series. The series explores the history behind important astronomical ideas alongside the outstanding mysteries that remain unsolved.

At UNSW, I contribute to the Physics Outreach Committee and have participated in UNSW Open Day, National Science Week events, Science in the Swamp, and Science in the Scrub.
